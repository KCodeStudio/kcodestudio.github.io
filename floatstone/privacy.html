2. The game at a glance

Floatstone is a one-touch arcade game for Android: hold to fall as a stone, release to float as a feather, and fall as deep as you can. The game is in English and is intended for players aged 13 and older. It is not directed at children under 13 and does not ask for your age.

Floatstone has no user accounts, no sign-in, no online leaderboards, no chat, no in-app purchases and no analytics of its own. K-Code Studio does not run a server that receives data from the game. The only online feature is the optional rewarded video described in section 4. The game can be played completely without watching a video.

3. Data stored on your device

The game saves your best score in the app's private storage on your device. Nothing else is saved by the game itself. Android cloud backup is disabled for Floatstone, so this file is not copied to your Google account by the game.

The Google advertising components (section 4) and Google's consent tool (section 5) may also store information on your device, for example your consent choices and technical data needed to show ads.

You can delete all of this by clearing the app's storage in Android's settings or by uninstalling the game. This does not delete data that Google has already received.

4. Rewarded video ads (Google AdMob)

Floatstone is free and is financed by one optional rewarded video. When you lose a run for the first time, you can choose to watch a short video to continue that run. If you choose “No thanks”, a new run starts and no video is shown. There are no banner ads and no ads that appear on their own.

The videos are provided by Google AdMob (Google Mobile Ads SDK), integrated with the Godot AdMob plugin by Poing Studios. When the game starts and advertising is allowed (see section 5), the SDK is initialized and one video is loaded in advance, so it is ready when you need it. This means the game communicates with Google even if you never watch a video.

To deliver ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google may process information such as your IP address, approximate location derived from it, device and app information, the Android advertising ID, ad interactions (for example whether a video was shown or completed) and diagnostic data. Whether ads are personalized depends on your consent choices (section 5) and the rules that apply in your region. Without consent, Google may still show non-personalized or limited ads, which also involve some processing, for example of the IP address.

Development and test versions of the game, and devices registered by the developer as test devices, receive Google's test ads instead of real ads. Test ads also involve communication with Google.

5. Consent and how to change it

When the game starts, it uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) to check whether your consent is required, for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. If it is, Google's consent form is shown, where you can accept, reject or adjust the use of your data for advertising.

The advertising SDK is only started after you have answered the form, or if no consent is required in your region. If the form cannot be shown or is not answered, no ads are loaded and the game simply does not offer the “Continue” video. If you reject personalized advertising, Google may still show non-personalized or limited ads, based on your choices.

You can change or withdraw your choice at any time using the “Privacy” option on the game's title screen, which opens Google's privacy options again. Clearing the app's storage also resets your choice, and the form is shown again on the next start. Withdrawing consent does not affect processing that happened before.

You can also reset or delete your Android advertising ID in your device settings (Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads, depending on your device).

6. Google as a recipient

For AdMob, Google acts as an independent controller of the data it receives. For users in Europe, this is Google Ireland Limited; other Google companies, including Google LLC in the USA, may also be involved. Google may therefore process data outside the European Union. Google describes the safeguards it uses for such transfers, including standard contractual clauses and the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, in the documents linked below.

How long Google keeps data is decided by Google according to its own retention rules. Uninstalling the game does not delete data that Google has already received; requests about that data may need to be sent to Google.

7. App permissions

Floatstone requests internet access and network-state access to load videos. The Google advertising components add the advertising-ID permission and a few technical permissions (for example to keep a download running). The game does not access your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files.

8. Legal bases

Where your consent is required, storing and reading information on your device for advertising and personalized advertising are based on your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and § 25(1) TDDDG). Saving your best score on your device is necessary to provide the game you are using (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR and § 25(2) TDDDG). Showing optional, non-personalized ads to finance a free game and handling support requests are based on our legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

9. Contacting support

If you email us, we receive your email address and your message and use them only to answer you. The support mailbox is hosted by Gmail, so Google also processes these emails as the mail provider. Your emails are deleted once your request has been fully resolved, unless a law requires us to keep them longer. Please do not send passwords or other sensitive information.

10. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correction, deletion, restriction of processing and data portability, the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. Because the game does not identify you and keeps only your best score on your device, we usually cannot link any Google advertising data to you; such requests may need to go to Google.
